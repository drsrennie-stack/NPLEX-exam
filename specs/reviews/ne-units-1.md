## Changes

| Unit | Field | What was wrong | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| NE-1 | notes "Who makes which cells", mesoderm bullet | Listed the dura mater as mesoderm. The spinal dura is mesodermal, but the forebrain meninges come partly from neural crest (Langman), so the flat claim is incomplete, and the objective asks only about microglia. | Removed the dura; bullet now covers microglia only. |
| NE-2 | notes "Neuron parts and shapes", pseudounipolar bullet | Said pseudounipolar neurons are in "cranial sensory ganglia", which contradicts the next bullet (spiral and vestibular ganglion neurons are bipolar). | Now "dorsal root ganglia and most cranial sensory ganglia". |
| NE-2 | chart "Neuron shapes", pseudounipolar row | Same contradiction as above. | Now "Dorsal root ganglia, most cranial sensory ganglia". |
| NE-4 | notes "Bleeds by space", epidural bullet | "Temporal bone fracture at the pterion": the pterion is the junction of the frontal, parietal, temporal, and sphenoid bones, not part of the temporal bone alone. | Now "a skull fracture at the pterion". |
| NE-4 | notes "Bleeds by space", subarachnoid bullet | Implied a berry aneurysm is the cause of all subarachnoid hemorrhage; trauma is the most common cause overall, and aneurysm is the usual cause of spontaneous bleeds. | Now "Spontaneous subarachnoid hemorrhage: usually a ruptured berry aneurysm". |
| NE-5 | chart "Headache types", tension Extras cell | "None" was too strong; ICHD criteria allow one of mild photophobia or phonophobia (no nausea). | Now "No nausea; at most mild light or sound sensitivity". |
| NE-5 | tricks "C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive" | Named the shoulder tip as the C4 dermatome only; referred diaphragm pain is taught as the C3 to C5 dermatomes (Moore), and sources differ on a single level. | Now "felt at the shoulder tip (C3 to C5 dermatomes)". |
| NE-5 | tricks "Pain crosses at once, touch crosses late" | "Cross right away" overstated it; spinothalamic fibers cross within one or two segments, as the notes say. | Now "cross within a segment or two of entering the cord". |
| NE-5 | pitfalls, postherpetic neuralgia line | Called acute shingles pain "acute inflammatory pain", setting it against neuropathic pain; acute zoster pain also has a neuropathic component, so the contrast was not accurate. | Now defines postherpetic neuralgia as pain lasting months after the rash heals, neuropathic, from nerve damage the virus left, not ongoing infection. |
| NE-6 | notes "Pupils", anisocoria bullet | Stated anisocoria is always an efferent problem; physiologic anisocoria is a common harmless variant. | Now "Anisocoria from disease is an efferent problem..." plus a note that a small difference can be a normal variant. |
