# Reviewer log: notes/chapters/HE-8.json (Blood-Borne Pathogens and the Red Cell)

Reviewed line by line against Jawetz, Robbins, Abbas, Lange Pathophysiology of Disease, Guyton and Hall, and First Aid level consensus. Checker: 0 errors, 0 warnings before and after. Prose 2,786 words (was 2,781), so the chapter was corrected without being lengthened.

## Facts corrected
- Overview connection mislabeled the companion units: "Hemoglobinopathies and enzymopathies (HE-4, HE-5)" changed to "Hemoglobin biochemistry and hematologic genetics (HE-5, HE-6)", since HE-4 is hematopoiesis and hemostasis and the genetics of these variants sits in HE-6.
- HE-8.1 overstated sporozoite fate ("cleared from plasma within about an hour, most of them into hepatocytes"); corrected to a fraction entering hepatocytes with the remainder destroyed, which is the consensus account.
- HE-8.1 species table called the P. vivax and P. ovale cycle "benign tertian"; that name belongs to vivax alone, so the cell now reads "About 48 h (tertian)".
- HE-8.2 placed PfEMP1 export "about halfway through" the cycle; corrected to about 12 to 16 hours into the 48 hour cycle, as the ring matures into a trophozoite, which is when knobs and surface PfEMP1 appear.
- HE-8.3 stated flatly that HbC and HbE are weaker than sickle trait; corrected to note that both protect by related effects on growth and knob-mediated adherence, with the largest reported reduction in homozygous hemoglobin C.
- HE-8.4 said Babesia is transmitted by transfusion of "red cells or platelets" without qualification; corrected to red cells, and rarely platelet units carrying residual red cells, since the infective unit is a parasitized erythrocyte.
- HE-8.4 described free merozoites as "haploid"; the word was accurate but irrelevant to the smear point and was removed to avoid implying a diagnostic feature.
- HE-8.6 worked example asserted a "normal bilirubin" in hereditary spherocytosis, where baseline bilirubin is usually mildly raised; changed to a bilirubin no higher than the patient's usual value, which preserves the reasoning and is accurate.

## Checked and left unchanged (verified correct)
- Two-host cycle with the female Anopheles as definitive host, exoerythrocytic schizogony setting the incubation period, 48 hour cycle in falciparum, vivax and ovale with 72 hours in P. malariae, and gametocytes as the non-multiplying infective stage.
- Hypnozoite relapse versus recrudescence versus reinfection, and the point that transfusion-transmitted vivax skips the liver and does not relapse.
- Cell-age preference by species (falciparum all ages, vivax reticulocytes, malariae senescent cells) and the quartan nephropathy entry.
- Falciparum invasion through sialic acid residues on glycophorins A, B and C plus sialic acid independent pathways; PfEMP1 binding to CD36, ICAM-1, EPCR and placental chondroitin sulfate A; about 60 var genes with epigenetically controlled switching; rosetting; hemozoin; banana-shaped gametocytes.
- G6PD as the rate-limiting hexose monophosphate shunt enzyme and the red cell's only NADPH source, and X inactivation mosaicism in heterozygous females.
- Duffy: PvDBP engaging ACKR1, erythroid-specific promoter mutation, near fixation in West and Central Africa, no hematologic cost, no effect on falciparum.
- Babesia: absence of a liver phase and of hypnozoites, Ixodes scapularis nymph with white-footed mouse reservoir, attachment longer than about a day, binary fission with asynchronous lysis, absence of hemozoin, Maltese cross tetrads, survival in refrigerated storage, and severity governed by splenic function and age.
- Schistosoma: separate sexes with the female in the gynecophoral canal, Biomphalaria, Oncomelania and Bulinus hosts, asexual amplification in the snail, cercarial skin penetration, migration through lungs and portal system, inferior versus superior mesenteric versus vesical plexus, egg spine morphology, Th2 granuloma with IL-4, IL-5 and IL-13, Symmers pipestem presinusoidal portal hypertension with preserved hepatocyte function, and squamous metaplasia to carcinoma in S. haematobium.
- Parvovirus B19: nonenveloped single-stranded DNA genome, P antigen (globoside) receptor, S phase dependence through reliance on host DNA polymerase, giant pronormoblasts, resistance of the p phenotype, roughly 1 g/dL fall over a 10 day production pause on a 120 day lifespan, contagiousness before the rash, and myocardial P antigen contributing to nonimmune hydrops.
