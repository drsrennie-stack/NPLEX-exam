## Facts corrected
- C05, myeloperoxidase deficiency: removed the claim that both the dihydrorhodamine and nitroblue tetrazolium tests are normal. The nitroblue tetrazolium test is normal, but oxidation of dihydrorhodamine 123 to rhodamine is peroxidase dependent, so severe myeloperoxidase deficiency can give a low dihydrorhodamine signal and mimic chronic granulomatous disease despite an intact burst.
- C05 table row on dihydrorhodamine and nitroblue tetrazolium rewritten to match that correction, and the caption now says the nitroblue tetrazolium test is what separates the two disorders.
