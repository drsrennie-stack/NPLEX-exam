## Changes

- IN-13-D, notes "Epidermolysis bullosa": mucosal and nail involvement was listed only under recessive dystrophic EB, but it occurs across many EB types (objective IN-13-D.2 lists it as part of the general course). Moved "mouth and nails are often involved" to the general EB bullet; the recessive dystrophic bullet now lists heavy scarring, mitten hands, nail loss, esophageal strictures, and SCC.
- IN-13-D, trick "Fishnet versus railroad": the label said "railroad" (two lines) while the text and the pemphigoid pattern describe a single linear band. Relabeled "Fishnet versus single line".
- IN-13-E, notes "Oculocutaneous albinism": said tyrosinase loss is "most often" the cause. OCA2 (P protein) is the most common form worldwide; tyrosinase loss (OCA1) is the classic teaching form. Changed to "Classic form (OCA1): loss of tyrosinase".
- IN-13-E, highYield: "absent melanin, usually tyrosinase loss" changed to "little or no melanin, classically tyrosinase loss" (some OCA types make reduced, not absent, pigment).
- IN-13-G, pitfalls: said acral and mucosal melanoma occur on skin that "never sees the sun". Palms and soles get some sun. Reworded to sites that get little or no sun.
- IN-13-H, trick "Rule of threes for needle sticks": stated 3% for hepatitis C and then contradicted itself. Rewritten with the consensus figures (HBV about 30% from an HBeAg-positive source, HCV about 2%, HIV about 0.3%) and the tenfold steps; still marked "trick".
- IN-13-H, coverage (objective IN-13-H.6): cutaneous and pilonidal abscess were not taught. Added to the folliculitis bullet (skin abscess, usually S aureus; pilonidal abscess in the sacrococcygeal cleft around trapped hair) and to the Folliculitis row of the infectious conditions chart. Bullet count kept at 35.
